# 5. Level 3: Completeness Theorem and Proof

## 5.1 Main Theorem

Let $P_3$ be the position set of Level 3, $S_3$ be the set of 3,591 atomic moves passing the current collision check, and $G_3=\langle S_3\rangle$ be the group they generate.

**Main Theorem (Constructive Solvability of Level 3).** For any state reachable from the solved state under the action of $G_3$, the algorithm `level3-slice-reduction` constructs a finite word of legal atomic moves returning the state to the solved state, assuming the computer executing the finite searches does not encounter resource failures such as running out of memory.

This theorem states not merely that a finite solving sequence exists, but that the implementation constructively produces it. Below, we decompose the proof into lemmas.

**Structure Theorem (Characterization of Reachable States, 2026-10-10).** A state is reachable if and only if the following four conditions hold:

1. Every cell lies within its own position orbit, and its orientation belongs to the set of orientations permitted for its current position (the coset of $H(\Omega)$ in Section 5.8).
2. The 164-bit orbit parity vector lies in the 15-dimensional subspace $V$ spanned by the parity vectors of the atomic moves.
3. In each of the four $C_3$ orbits, the sum of twists is $0\pmod 3$.
4. In the $D_4$ orbit, the number of flipped cells is even.

Consequently, the induced position group is $P=\{\pi\in\prod_i\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega_i):\varepsilon(\pi)\in V\}$, and the group order is

$$
|G_3|=2^{15}\prod_{i=1}^{164}\frac{|\Omega_i|!}{2}\cdot3^{28}\cdot4^{552}\cdot\frac{8^{192}}{2},
\qquad\log_{10}|G_3|\approx11918.138
$$

Conditions 3 and 4 are the direct analogues of the classical Rubik's Cube invariants ("the sum of corner twists is zero" and "the number of edge flips is even"). The full proof is in `docs/proofs/level3-completeness.tex`.

**Revision Note (2026-10-10).** Earlier versions deduced the existence of "3-cycles that do not disturb cells outside the orbit" in Lemma 9 directly from Lemma 6, and assumed in Lemma 10 that "an operation decreasing the orientation distance can always be found." While both conclusions hold, their proofs were previously incomplete. This version adds Lemmas 7a and 7b along with Lemmas 10a and 10b, and incorporates a guaranteed-terminating completion phase into the implementation.

**Scope of Applicability (2026-09 Addendum).** This theorem applies to the group $G_3$ generated by $S_3$. "Inner-square rotations of square cross-sections" outside the standard candidate generator family (for example, rotating the 4 cells at $(\pm5,\pm5,0)$ in the $z=0$ plane around the Z-axis through the origin) also pass the physical collision detector, but are not included in $S_3$. Completeness for the expanded group including those moves does not follow immediately from this theorem alone (see `docs/research/square-region-rotation-impact.md` §9 for the initial scope note, and `docs/proofs/inner-square-completeness.md` for the subsequent inner-square study).

## 5.2 Complete Decomposition of Position Orbits

Constructing the graph of Lemma 3 from the position action of the 3,591 atomic moves partitions the 8,000 positions into 164 orbits:

| Orbit size | Number of orbits | Number of cells |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | 4 | 32 |
| 24 | 112 | 2,688 |
| 96 | 45 | 4,320 |
| 192 | 1 | 192 |
| 384 | 2 | 768 |
| **Total** | **164** | **8,000** |

The total number of distinct ordered pairs within the orbits is

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{164}|\Omega_i|(|\Omega_i|-1)=803264
$$

This is roughly $1/80$ of $8000\cdot7999=63992000$, which would be the count if all 8,000 positions could be freely permuted into one another.

## 5.4 Every Orbit Contains All Even Permutations

Let $G_3^\Omega$ denote the permutation group induced by $G_3$ on an orbit $\Omega$.

**Lemma 6.** For all 164 orbits,

$$
\operatorname{Alt}(\Omega)\le G_3^\Omega
$$

**Computer-Assisted Proof.** For the four orbits of size 8, the induced group was enumerated to closure and verified to equal $\operatorname{Sym}(8)$ of order $8!=40320$.

For each orbit of size 24 or greater, the following certificate was verified:

1. No nontrivial block system preserved by the generators exists; that is, the action is primitive. This is verified by computing the closure of the minimal congruence relation placing each pair of points into the same block, and confirming that only the trivial partition (singletons) or the universal partition (the whole set) is obtained.
2. From the cycle decomposition of a generator word $g$, a power $g^K$ is taken and directly verified to have support consisting of a single cycle of prime length $p$.
3. The inequality $p\le |\Omega|-3$ is verified.

By Jordan's theorem—which states that a primitive finite permutation group containing a prime-length cycle satisfying this bound contains the alternating group—the conclusion follows. Randomness is used only to search for candidate words $g$; the final verification is an exact, deterministic inspection of the resulting explicit permutation. All 164 orbits are thus certified. $\square$

## 5.5 Globally Pure Tools for All Orbits

**Lemma 7.** For each orbit $\Omega_i$, there exists a nontrivial even-permutation tool $T_i\in G_3$ that fixes both the position and orientation of every cell outside $\Omega_i$.

The distribution of support sizes is as follows:

| Tool support size | Number of orbits |
| ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 133 |
| 4 | 17 |
| 6 | 14 |

Of these, 117 tools were obtained from the interchange of two small-support commutators. The remaining 47 tools were obtained via **conjugate separation**, taking the commutator of a tool $T$ with a conjugate $gTg^{-1}$ to cancel common actions outside the target orbit.

Each tool word has length between 16 and 84 atomic moves, with a median of 16. When loading the stored tool data, the following properties are re-verified across all 8,000 positions:

- Every atomic move ID resolves to a currently legal atomic move.
- All moved positions lie strictly within the declared orbit.
- Cells whose positions are fixed suffer no orientation side effects.
- The support size is at least 3.

The independent audit succeeded on all 164/164 orbits, with 0 out-of-orbit actions, 0 fixed-cell twists, and 0 degenerate tools. $\square$

**Lemma 7a (Pure 3-Cycles).** For every one of the 164 orbits, there exists an explicit word that cycles exactly three points of the orbit while leaving the position and orientation of all other cells unchanged.

For 133 orbits, the stored Lemma 7 tool is already a pure 3-cycle. For the 31 orbits whose base tool has support size 4 or 6, a product of two conjugates of the tool that moves exactly three points was found and saved as an explicit word (ranging from 42 to 170 atomic moves). All 164 words are verified across all 8,000 cells. $\square$

**Lemma 7b (Pure Realization of the Alternating Group).** For any three distinct points $x,y,z$ in any orbit $\Omega$, there exists an element of $G_3$ that cycles $x\to y\to z\to x$ and leaves everything else unchanged. Consequently, any $\alpha\in\operatorname{Alt}(\Omega)$ can be realized by an element of $G_3$ that leaves all cells outside $\Omega$ unchanged.

**Proof.** By Lemma 6, $G_3^\Omega$ acts transitively on ordered triples of distinct points in $\Omega$, so there exists $u\in G_3$ mapping $(x,y,z)$ to the three support points of the pure 3-cycle tool $T$ from Lemma 7a. Then $u\,T\,u^{-1}$ is the required element. Any cell outside $\{x,y,z\}$ is mapped by $u$ outside the support of $T$ and then mapped back by $u^{-1}$, so its orientation is also unchanged. $\square$

Note that Lemma 6 alone does not imply Lemma 7b, because Lemma 6 is a statement about the induced permutation group on $\Omega$ and does not guarantee that cells outside $\Omega$ remain undisturbed. Group-theoretically, the pointwise stabilizer $K_\Omega$ of the complement of $\Omega$ is a normal subgroup of $G_3$; its image in $G_3^\Omega$ is a nontrivial normal subgroup and therefore contains $\operatorname{Alt}(\Omega)$.

## 5.6 Parity Normalization

Let $\varepsilon(s)$ denote the 164-bit orbit parity vector of a state $s\in G_3$. The 3,591 atomic moves yield 20 distinct nonzero parity vectors, whose linear span over $\mathbf F_2$ has rank 15.

**Lemma 8.** The orbit parities of any reachable state can be normalized to all-even in at most 15 atomic moves.

**Proof.** Since $s\in\langle S_3\rangle$, $\varepsilon(s)$ lies in the linear span of $\{\varepsilon(a):a\in S_3\}$. Gaussian elimination with respect to a basis of rank 15 expresses $\varepsilon(s)$ as a sum of at most 15 basis vectors. Applying the inverse atomic moves corresponding to those basis vectors reduces the parity vector to $0$. Since the basis has 15 elements, at most 15 atomic moves are required. $\square$

All subsequent pure tools are constructed from commutators; by Lemma 1, they induce even permutations on every orbit and therefore preserve the normalized parities.

## 5.7 Position Solver

Each orbit is solved in two phases. Every tool application is a conjugate of the pure 3-cycle from Lemma 7a.

- **Greedy Phase (Heuristic).** While more than 32 cells in the orbit remain unsolved, the solver searches via breadth-first search on the finite graph of ordered pairs $(x,y)\in\Omega^2,x\ne y$ for a conjugate that solves strictly more cells than it displaces. If none is found, it transitions to the exact phase.
- **Exact Phase.** The remaining even permutation is decomposed into 3-cycles by Lemma 4, and each 3-cycle is constructed via bidirectional breadth-first search between the target ordered triple and the support triple of the stored pure 3-cycle.

**Lemma 9.** After parity normalization, the position solver reaches $\sigma=1$ in a finite number of steps.

**Proof.** Each step of the greedy phase strictly increases the number of solved cells and thus terminates in at most $|\Omega|$ steps; because every applied word is even on $\Omega$, the residual permutation at the start of the exact phase is even. Each step of the exact phase solves at least two remaining cells (or the final three) and thus also terminates in at most $|\Omega|$ steps. The required 3-cycles exist by Lemma 7b. By Lemma 6, the graph of ordered triples is connected, and since the search has no depth cutoff, a conjugating setup word is guaranteed to be found. Because the pure tools fix everything outside the current orbit, previously solved orbits are never disturbed. Processing all 164 orbits in sequence completes all positions. $\square$

## 5.8 Orientation Freedom

Exhaustive exploration of the $\Omega\times C$ automaton for each orbit reveals that in 148 orbits (comprising 7,224 cells), the orientation freedom at the home position is 1: once the position is solved, the orientation is automatically solved. Only 16 orbits (776 cells) have nontrivial orientation freedom:

| Group | Orbits | Cells | Single-cell roll | Orbit-wide constraint |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| $C_3$ | 4 | 32 | No | Sum of twists is $0\pmod 3$ |
| $C_4$ | 11 | 552 | Yes (all cells) | None |
| $D_4$ | 1 | 192 | Yes (rotational subgroup on all cells) | Number of flips is even |

The pointwise orientation freedoms alone do not determine the full orientation group; there are simultaneous orbit-wide constraints.

**Lemma 10a (Invariance).** All 3,591 atomic moves preserve the twist sum ($\bmod\ 3$) on each of the four $C_3$ orbits and the parity of the number of flipped cells on the $D_4$ orbit. Consequently, both invariants are $0$ in every reachable state.

**Lemma 10b (Pointwise Position-Stabilizer Subgroup).** The subgroup of elements fixing the positions of all cells coincides precisely with the set of orientation configurations satisfying the invariants of Lemma 10a, and has order $3^{28}\cdot4^{552}\cdot8^{192}/2$.

**Proof Sketch.** All 744 cells in the $C_4$ and $D_4$ orbits possess legal single-cell rotations, which independently generate all rotational orientations in those orbits. For each $C_3$ orbit, an explicit 68-move word fixing all positions and twisting exactly two cells by $(c,-c)$ is stored; for the $D_4$ orbit, an explicit 32-move word flipping exactly two cells while fixing all positions is stored. By the 2-transitivity established in Lemma 6, these two-cell orientation tools can be conjugated to any pair of cells within their respective orbits. $\square$

The orientation solver proceeds in two phases:

- **Descent Phase (Heuristic).** Orientation tools are formed from single-cell rotations, quotients of pure tools with identical position action, $[R,T]$-type commutators, and 13 dedicated words for $D_4$. The solver searches for applications that strictly decrease the sum $\Phi$ of distances to the solved orientation. When no such application is found, it transitions to the completion phase.
- **Completion Phase.** (1) All cells that can be solved by single-cell rotations are aligned directly. (2) In each $C_3$ orbit, while two or more twisted cells remain, a conjugate of the two-cell twist tool is applied once or twice to eliminate at least one twisted cell. (3) In the $D_4$ orbit, flipped cells are paired up and unflipped two at a time using conjugates of the two-cell flip tool, followed by single-cell rotations to finish the rotational alignment.

**Lemma 10.** Any reachable orientation state after position completion is solved in a finite number of steps.

**Proof.** The descent phase strictly decreases $\Phi\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}$ at every step and therefore terminates in finitely many steps. Step (1) of the completion phase completely solves all $C_4$ orbits and reduces the $D_4$ orbit to pure flips. Step (2) decreases the number of twisted cells in each $C_3$ orbit by at least 1 per iteration, and because the twist sum is $0\pmod 3$, a single isolated twisted cell can never remain. Step (3) decreases the number of flipped cells by 2 per iteration, and the number of flipped cells is even. The required conjugating setup words exist by 2-transitivity and are guaranteed to be found by the uncutoff breadth-first search. $\square$

For unreachable inputs, the solver explicitly identifies the violated invariant and terminates with a failure report.

## 5.9 Proof of the Main Theorem

**Proof.** Because the input state is reachable, its orbit parities can be normalized in at most 15 moves by Lemma 8. By Lemma 9, all cell positions are solved in finitely many steps. By Lemma 10, all cell orientations are solved in finitely many steps while keeping all positions fixed. Every word used consists solely of legal atomic moves from $S_3$. Therefore, the constructed finite word transforms the input state into the solved state. $\square$

## 5.10 Boundary Verification of Soundness

Although the core search uses a fast sparse integer simulator, the final success verdict does not rely on it. The input state is cloned, and the output sequence is replayed move-by-move in the actual puzzle engine. Legality is checked immediately prior to each move, and at the end of the sequence, exact agreement of both position and orientation is required across all 8,000 cells. If even a single move is illegal or a single cell disagrees, the solver returns `success: false`.

Thus, even if an implementation discrepancy existed in the sparse search simulator, any false solution would be rejected at the verification boundary. Furthermore, the sparse simulator and the actual engine have passed differential testing across 6 trials × 40 legal atomic moves × 8,000 cells for both position and orientation.

## 5.11 Move Counts and God's Number

Across seven benchmark trials, solutions required between 111,830 and 424,519 moves and between 112.1 and 284.2 seconds (measured on the implementation prior to the 2026-10-10 revision). These are not shortest-path move counts.

Counting one legal atomic move as one move, the number of words of length at most $d$ is at most

$$
1+M+\cdots+M^d=\frac{M^{d+1}-1}{M-1},\qquad M=3591
$$

Meanwhile, the product of alternating groups on the position orbits alone contains

$$
\prod_{i=1}^{164}|\operatorname{Alt}(|\Omega_i|)|
=\prod_{i=1}^{164}\frac{|\Omega_i|!}{2}
$$

states, whose base-10 logarithm is approximately $11394.834$. From this subgroup alone, we obtain $G_3\ge3206$. Using the full Structure Theorem increases the number of counted states and sharpens the lower bound:

| Counted state space | $\log_{10}$ | Lower bound on moves |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $\prod_i\operatorname{Alt}(\Omega_i)$ | 11394.834 | 3206 |
| Position group $P$ | 11399.349 | 3207 |
| Full group $G_3$ | 11918.138 | 3353 |

Consequently, we obtain the information-theoretic lower bound

$$
G_3\ge3353
$$

The long move sequences produced by the current solver are outputs of a non-optimal constructive algorithm and do not represent a tight upper bound on God's number.
