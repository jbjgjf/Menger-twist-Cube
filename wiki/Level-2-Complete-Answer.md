# 4. Level 2: Constructive Solver and Previously Open Parts

## 4.1 Distinguishing Two Models

Before discussing the completeness of Level 2, we distinguish two groups generated by different generating sets:

- $G_2^{\mathrm{abs}}$: The legacy abstract model permitting all 324 syntactically generated rotation targets, i.e., 972 atomic moves.
- $G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}$: The current physical model permitting only the 156 targets that pass continuous-rotation collision detection, i.e., 468 atomic moves.

These two groups satisfy the subgroup relation

$$
G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}\le G_2^{\mathrm{abs}}
$$

However, tools constructed for the larger group cannot necessarily be constructed within the smaller group.

**Addendum (2026-10, Completeness Proved).** The reachable states of $G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}$ have been completely determined, and the completeness of the solver `level2-certified` has been proved (`docs/proofs/level2-completeness.tex`). All required conditions in Section 4.7 have been satisfied. Note, however, that the subject of the proof is `level2-certified`; the completeness of `level2-slice-reduction` described below remains unproved. The remainder of this chapter is preserved as a record of the development prior to that proof.

**Status Proposition (as of 2026-09).** The implementation `level2-slice-reduction` solves numerous scrambles in $G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}$, and the returned solutions are verified by replay in the actual engine. However, completeness over all reachable states of $G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}$ is unproved.

Below, we first rigorously describe the construction obtained in the legacy abstract model, and finally identify the parts that had not yet been transferred to the current physical model.

## 4.2 Five Coordinate Classes

Write each position as $p=3b+o$. Each digit $b,o\in\{-1,0,1\}^3$ has at most one zero component. A digit with no zero components is called corner-type (`C`), and a digit with one zero component is called edge-type (`E`).

When both digits are `E`, positions where the zero-component axes of $b$ and $o$ coincide are denoted `EEa` (aligned), and those where the axes differ are denoted `EEo` (oblique).

| class | Condition | Cells |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| CC | $z(b)=0,z(o)=0$ | 64 |
| CE | $z(b)=0,z(o)=1$ | 96 |
| EC | $z(b)=1,z(o)=0$ | 96 |
| EEa | $z(b)=z(o)=1$, zero axes aligned | 48 |
| EEo | $z(b)=z(o)=1$, zero axes distinct | 96 |

The total number of cells is $64+96+96+48+96=400$.

**Lemma 5 (Class Invariance).** Every atomic move of the legacy abstract model preserves the five classes above.

**Proof Sketch.** Frame rotations map affected digits by the same coordinate rotation. Extensions rotate only the local digit. Coordinate-axis rotations preserve the number of zero components in each digit and also preserve whether the two zero axes coincide. Therefore, all five conditions are invariant. In the implementation, zero counterexamples were confirmed across all 972 atomic moves and all 400 positions. $\square$

Decomposing the position action of the legacy model via union-find yields 4 orbits each for `CC` and `CE`, and 1 orbit each for `EC`, `EEa`, and `EEo`, giving 11 orbits in total. Classes alone are too coarse as units for solving; actual tools operate within individual orbits.

## 4.3 Orientation Freedom in the Legacy Model

Exhaustive exploration of the $\Omega\times C$ automaton for each orbit yields the following results:

| class | Reachable orientations at home position | Group type | Meaning for solving |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| CC | 3 | $C_3$ | 3-way corner twist |
| CE | 4 | $C_4$ | 4-way rotation about the slot axis |
| EC | 1 | Trivial group | Position uniquely determines orientation |
| EEa | 8 | $D_4$ | 4 rotations and reflection (flip) |
| EEo | 4 | $C_4$ | 4-way rotation about the slot axis |

Here $C_k$ denotes the cyclic group generated by a single $k$-fold rotation, and $D_4$ denotes the 8-element dihedral group of rotations and reflections of a square.

**Corollary.** In the legacy model, once an `EC` cell is placed in its correct position, its orientation is automatically correct.

This follows immediately from the fact that the orientation freedom is 1, meaning no orientation other than the identity is reachable at the home position.

## 4.4 Construction of Tools

### 4.4.1 Edge-Region Tools

Let $S$ be a slice move and $E$ be a depth-1 extension or slab move. Since $E$ moves only edge blocks, the action of the commutator $[S,E]$ on the corner region cancels out. Composing two such small-support commutators with appropriately overlapping supports yields a pure permutation acting solely within a single class (`EC`, `EEa`, or `EEo`).

Typical words have length 16 in atomic moves. The cycle types of their supports include not only 3-cycles, but also 5-cycles, double 3-cycles, 7-cycles, and double 4-cycles. Because commutators are even permutations, odd permutations such as a single 4-cycle never appear.

### 4.4.2 Corner-Region Tools

By conjugating two slice-type operations and forming their commutator, we obtain tools that move only `CC` cells or only `CE` cells. These tools are allowed to disturb the edge region, because the solver processes corners first and edges afterward.

### 4.4.3 Orientation Tools

If $T_1,T_2$ are two tools with identical position action but distinct orientation actions, then $T_1T_2^{-1}$ is a position-identity orientation tool. Furthermore, the commutator $[R,T]$ of a single-cell rotation $R$ and a position tool $T$ transports the action of $R$ to another cell within the support of $T$, altering the orientations of only a small number of cells.

## 4.5 Solver for the Legacy Model

**Algorithm L2-A.**

1. If every 3×3×3 block is rigid, project to the 20-macro-cell Level 1 state and solve it.
2. Compute the 11-orbit parity vector and reduce all components to 0 by solving a system of linear equations over $\mathbf F_2$ in the atomic move parity vectors.
3. Solve `CC` positions using corner tools.
4. Solve `CE` positions using tools that preserve `CC`.
5. Correct `CC` orientations using position-identity orientation tools.
6. Solve `EC` positions using tools that fix the corner region. The `EC` orientations are then automatically aligned.
7. Solve `EEa` and `EEo` positions using pure edge tools.
8. Correct `CE`, `EEa`, and `EEo` orientations using single-cell rotations and orientation commutators.
9. Replay the entire output word in the actual engine.

**Completeness Argument for the Legacy Model.** After parity normalization, the remaining position permutation on each orbit is even. Each orbit can be solved using pure 3-cycles and conjugation. Because the corner region is processed first and subsequent edge tools fix the corners, the already-solved portions are preserved. For orientations, orientation tools generating the full orientation freedom found by the finite automaton are used to strictly decrease the sum of distances to the solved orientation. Consequently, as long as the required searches exhaustively explore the finite graphs without truncation, the procedure terminates.

Note that this construction presupposes the availability of tool words valid in the legacy abstract model.

## 4.6 Assumptions Lost in the Current Physical Model

Among the 240 candidate depth-2 single-cell extensions, only 72 targets are physically legal; 168 targets are excluded due to collisions during continuous rotation. The tool library of the legacy implementation was built from a candidate set that included these excluded atomic moves.

This restriction can affect any of the following:

1. The 11 orbits might split into finer orbits.
2. The orientation freedoms $C_3,C_4,D_4$ might shrink.
3. The subspace spanned by the orbit parity vectors might change.
4. Illegal atomic moves might appear within the words of position or orientation tools.
5. Legacy tools might not be replaceable by alternative legal words.

Because the implementation rejects illegal moves or unsolved states during the final replay in the actual engine, it never returns a false positive. However, because it can fail on specific states, completeness cannot be claimed for that solver without further proof.

## 4.7 Required Conditions to Establish Completeness in the Current Model

All of the following must be re-executed using only the 468 legal atomic moves and saved as verifiable certificates:

1. Re-decomposition of position orbits.
2. Proof that the induced position group on each orbit contains the required alternating group of even permutations.
3. Basis computation for the image of the parity homomorphism.
4. Globally pure position tools for every orbit.
5. Orientation freedom via the $\Omega\times C$ automaton.
6. Legal position-identity orientation tools generating every nontrivial orientation freedom.
7. Termination audit removing heuristic search cutoffs from position and orientation searches.
8. Differential testing between the sparse simulator and the actual engine.

Only when all of these are closed can a completeness theorem for Level 2 be stated in the same form as for Level 3.

**Addendum (2026-10).** All 8 items above have been closed in `docs/proofs/level2-completeness.tex`. The correspondence is as follows:

| Item | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Position orbits | 11 orbits (Check C2) |
| 2. Inclusion of alternating group | Transitivity on ordered triples verified by exhaustive search in every orbit (Check C7). Combined with a pure 3-cycle, this yields every 3-cycle |
| 3. Image of parity map | Dimension 5 (Check C5) |
| 4. Pure position tools | 8-move pure 3-cycles using only single-layer frame moves for all 11 orbits (Check C6) |
| 5. Orientation freedom | $C_3$ (two 8-cell orbits), $C_4$ (one 24-cell orbit), $D_4$ (the 48-cell orbit), and trivial on all others (Check C3) |
| 6. Orientation tools | Two-cell twist and flip tools (20 moves each) and single-cell rotations (Checks C6, C8) |
| 7. Termination | All searches are exhaustive without cutoffs; the number of solved cells strictly increases at each step |
| 8. Simulator differential check | Output is replayed move-by-move with legality checks in the caller's model |

Furthermore, the answers to the concerns raised in Section 4.6 are as follows: the orbits do not split (remaining 11); the orientation freedoms do shrink (`EEo` becomes trivial, and only a subset of the `CC` and `CE` orbits retain nontrivial orientation freedom); and the number of reachable states is $|G|\approx2.66\times10^{575}$.

**Relation to the Expanded Model (2026-09 Addendum).** Separately from $G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}$ above, an expanded model that exhaustively includes inner-square rotations (rotating a single-layer square cross-section as a unit) yields 54 additional legal atomic moves. Letting $H$ be the group generated by including these 54 additional moves, we proved with a replay-verified certificate that $[H:G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}]=2$. Consequently, completeness for $H$ reduces precisely to "completeness for $G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}$ preceded by at most one initial move." (At the time of the 2026-09 note, completeness of $G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}$ itself had not yet been proved, though it was subsequently closed in 2026-10; moreover, the same audit measured Items 1, 3, and 5—orbits, parity image, and orientation freedom—for $G_2^{\mathrm{phys}}$.) For details, see `docs/research/square-region-rotation-impact.md`.

## 4.8 Meaning of Performance Metrics

On deep random scrambles, the heuristic implementation (`level2-slice-reduction`) typically succeeds in roughly 2,200 to 2,800 moves within a few seconds. On rigid-block scrambles, it takes about 25 moves. While these are useful experimental results, they neither generalize to uninspected states nor constitute a claim of optimality.
