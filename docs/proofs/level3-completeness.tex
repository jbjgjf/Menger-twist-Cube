\title{Constructive Solvability of the Level 3 Menger Twist Cube}
\author{Menger Cube Project}
\date{October 10, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
The Level 3 Menger Twist Cube has 8,000 labeled cells. We show that the
\code{level3-slice-reduction} implementation returns every state reachable
under its 3,591 legal atomic moves to the solved state in finitely many moves.
The proof combines orbit decomposition, alternating-group actions,
orbit-local 3-cycles, parity normalization, and orientation invariants.
We characterize the reachable states and determine the order of the move group.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model}
A Level $n$ grid has side length $3^n$. Write each position uniquely in
balanced ternary as
\[
p=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}3^j d_j,\quad d_j\in\{-1,0,1\}^3,\qquad
P_n=\{p:z(d_j)\le1\text{ for every }j\},
\]
where $z(d)$ counts the zero coordinates. Thus $|P_n|=20^n$. At Level 3
write $p=9B+3b+o$.
Let $C$ be the 24-element rotation group of the cube. A state
$s=(\sigma,\rho)$ assigns each cell, labeled by its home $x\in P_3$,
a position $\sigma(x)$ and an orientation $\rho(x)\in C$.
The solved state has $\sigma=\mathrm{id}$ and $\rho(x)=I$.

An atomic move specifies a target $D\subseteq P_n$, an axis $a$, a point
$c$ on the axis, and an angle $\theta\in\{90^\circ,-90^\circ,180^\circ\}$.
On selected cells it sends $p$ to $c+R_{a,\theta}(p-c)$ and multiplies
orientations on the left by $R_{a,\theta}$. Cells are closed unit cubes.
The collision test permits boundary contact and excludes positive-volume
overlap between moving and stationary cells during the rotation.

The three angles for each legal target give $|S_3|=1197\cdot3=3591$
atomic moves. Let $G_3=\langle S_3\rangle$. Reachability means
reachability from the solved state by a word in $S_3$. We apply words
from left to right. For example, $uTu^{-1}$ transports the support of $T$
back through $u$. A commutator $ABA^{-1}B^{-1}$ is even because the sign
map is multiplicative.

The scope is the standard generator set. For example, rotating the four
cells $(\pm5,\pm5,0)$ about the $z$ axis through the origin passes the
collision test but is outside $S_3$.

\section{Main results}
\begin{theorem}[Constructive solvability]\label{thm:main}
For every reachable Level 3 state, \code{level3-slice-reduction} constructs
a finite word of legal atomic moves that solves it, provided that the
computer has sufficient resources for the finite searches.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Reachability and group order]\label{thm:structure}
A state is reachable if and only if it meets all four conditions below.
The orientation coordinates and maps are defined in Section~\ref{sec:ori}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The position permutation preserves every orbit $\Omega$, and
$\tau_s(x)\in H(\Omega)$ for each $x\in\Omega$.
\item The orbit parity vector $\varepsilon(\sigma)$ lies in the
15-dimensional subspace $V\subseteq\mathbb F_2^{164}$ spanned by the
atomic move parity vectors.
\item On each of the four $C_3$ orbits, $\sum_{x\in\Omega}\chi(\tau_s(x))=0$
in $\mathbb Z/3$.
\item On the single $D_4$ orbit, $\sum_{x\in\Omega}\varphi(\tau_s(x))=0$
in $\mathbb Z/2$.
\end{enumerate}
The position image is
$P=\{\pi\in\prod_i\Sym(\Omega_i):\varepsilon(\pi)\in V\}$, and
\[
|G_3|=\left(2^{15}\prod_{i=1}^{164}\frac{|\Omega_i|!}{2}\right)
       3^{28}4^{552}\frac{8^{192}}2,\qquad
\log_{10}|G_3|\approx11918.138.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:alt}
The induced position group $G_3^\Omega$ contains $\Alt(\Omega)$ on
every orbit $\Omega$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Computer-assisted proof]
For the four size-eight orbits, closure enumeration yields $8!=40320$
elements. On each larger orbit, verify primitivity by closing the
smallest generator-invariant equivalence relation identifying each pair
of distinct points. Each closure has one class. Then verify a word $g$
and an exponent $k$ such that $g^k$ acts as a single prime-length
$p$-cycle with $p\le|\Omega|-3$. Jordan's theorem gives the conclusion.
Randomness is used to find candidate words. The final check evaluates
the actual permutations.
\end{proof}

Call a word pure on an orbit if it fixes positions and orientations
outside the orbit and leaves orientations unchanged at every site it
fixes. The stored nontrivial even tools have support sizes 3, 4, and 6
on 133, 17, and 14 orbits respectively. Of these, 117 were obtained by
combining small-support commutators and 47 by commutators with conjugates.
The words have lengths 16--84, with median 16. All 164 passed an audit
of move legality, support, and orientation at fixed sites.

\begin{lemma}[Pure 3-cycles]\label{lem:pure}
Every orbit has a pure 3-cycle given by an explicit legal word.
Every ordered triple of distinct sites on that orbit can be cycled
by a pure word.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The stored size-three tools cover 133 orbits. On the other 31,
the original tool is a double transposition or a product of two
3-cycles. Breadth-first search over setup words found two conjugates
whose product has support exactly three. The resulting 42--170 move
words are stored in \code{level3SliceReductionCertifiedData.ts}.
Check C5 evaluates all 164 words on all 8,000 positions and orientations.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:alt} and $|\Omega|\ge8$, the induced group acts
transitively on ordered triples of distinct sites. Choose $u$ sending
the desired triple to the stored one, apply the stored cycle $T$, and
undo $u$. Outside the three chosen sites, $T$ acts trivially after
transport, and $u^{-1}$ restores both position and orientation.
\end{proof}
Every even permutation is a product of 3-cycles: shorten long
cycles using triples sharing their first point and express each
remaining pair of transpositions as at most two 3-cycles.
Lemma~\ref{lem:pure} therefore realizes any even permutation of
one orbit without disturbing other orbits. Containment of
$\Alt(\Omega)$ in the induced group alone gives no such independence.

\section{Parity normalization and position solving}
The signs on the 164 orbits define a homomorphism
$\varepsilon:G_3\to\mathbb F_2^{164}$, with bit one for odd sign.
The atomic move vectors give 20 distinct nonzero vectors of rank 15.
Gaussian elimination selects at most 15 atomic moves whose vectors
sum to the input parity. Applying their inverses makes each orbit even.

The solver processes one orbit at a time. While more than 32 sites
remain misplaced, it uses a breadth-first search on ordered pairs
to find a conjugate of a pure 3-cycle that increases the number of
correct sites. It switches to the exact phase when no improvement is
found. The number of correct sites bounds the number of greedy steps.
In the exact phase, the solver decomposes the remaining even
permutation into 3-cycles. It constructs each required conjugate by
bidirectional breadth-first search on ordered triples, from the
requested triple and the stored one. Lemma~\ref{lem:pure} supplies
a path, and exhaustive search of this finite graph terminates.
Each shortening step solves at least two sites, or four sites when
removing a pair of disjoint transpositions. Every completed orbit
stays solved in position.

\section{Orientation}\label{sec:ori}
\subsection{Local coordinates}
Fix a base site $x_\Omega$ in each orbit. Let $Q_y$ be the set of
orientations the base cell can have at $y$ and put
$H(\Omega)=Q_{x_\Omega}$. The latter is a subgroup of $C$ by
composition and inversion of loops. Choose $r_y\in Q_y$. Following
a reference path to $y$ after a loop gives $r_yH(\Omega)$.
Returning along the reference path shows the reverse inclusion, so
$Q_y=r_yH(\Omega)$. Exhaustive search on $\Omega\times C$ computes
these sets.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrll}
\toprule
$H(\Omega)$ & Orbits & Cells & Single-cell turns & Constraint\\
\midrule
Trivial&148&7224& None & Trivial orientation\\
$C_3$&4&32& None & Zero twist sum\\
$C_4$&11&552& Every site & None\\
$D_4$&1&192& Rotation subgroup & Even flip count\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Write $\pi_a$ for the position action and $R_a(p)$ for the applied
rotation at $p$ (identity when $p$ is unselected). Define
\[
\tau_s(x)=r_{\sigma(x)}^{-1}\rho(x)r_x,\qquad
\delta(a,p)=r_{\pi_a(p)}^{-1}R_a(p)r_p .
\]
Since $R_a(p)r_p\in Q_{\pi_a(p)}$, we have $\delta(a,p)\in H(\Omega)$.
Substitution gives $\tau_{s\cdot a}(x)=\delta(a,\sigma(x))\tau_s(x)$.
Starting from the solved state gives $\tau_s(x)\in H(\Omega)$ for
every reachable state. For a word $g$, multiply the successive
increments in reverse chronological order to obtain $\delta(g,p)$.
In particular $\delta(g^{-1},\pi_g(p))=\delta(g,p)^{-1}$.
If $g$ fixes every position, applying $u$, then $g$, then $u^{-1}$ gives
\[
\delta(ugu^{-1},p)=
\delta(u,p)^{-1}\delta(g,\pi_u(p))\delta(u,p).
\]
Thus transport conjugates the increment within $H(\Omega)$.

\subsection{Invariants and orientation tools}
On a $C_3$ orbit fix an isomorphism $\chi:H(\Omega)\to\mathbb Z/3$.
On the $D_4$ orbit let $C(\Omega)$ be the order-four rotation subgroup
and let $\varphi:H(\Omega)\to H(\Omega)/C(\Omega)\cong\mathbb Z/2$ be
the quotient map. For each atomic move, Check C6 verifies that the
sum of its local increments under $\chi$ or $\varphi$, as appropriate,
is zero. Because the maps are homomorphisms and $\sigma$ permutes
each orbit, applying a move changes the sum by exactly the sum of
its increments. Thus the sums are invariant and vanish in reachable states.

Check C7 verifies that on all 744 sites in the $C_4$ and $D_4$ orbits,
single-cell turns realize all three nonidentity rotations in the
local rotation subgroup. Check C8 verifies these additional words:
\begin{itemize}
\item On each $C_3$ orbit a 68-move word $P_\Omega$ fixes every position
and adds opposite nonzero twists at two sites, leaving all other
orientations fixed. It is obtained as $T u T^{-1}u^{-1}$, where $T$
is a pure cycle and $u$ cyclically permutes its support.
\item On the $D_4$ orbit a 32-move word $F$ fixes every position and
flips two orientations while leaving the rest fixed. This word is
one of 13 previously stored $D_4$ tools.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The orientation kernel}
Let $K$ be the kernel of the position action. The local orientation
condition and the sums above constrain $K$ to the product, over orbits,
of the zero-sum $C_3$ vectors, all $C_4$ vectors, the even-flip $D_4$
vectors, and the trivial factors.

For the reverse inclusion, single-cell turns generate the $C_4$
factors. On a $C_3$ orbit, the induced group is transitive on ordered
pairs by Lemma~\ref{lem:alt}. Transport $P_\Omega$ to any ordered pair.
Conjugation preserves its increments since $C_3$ is abelian.
Such transfers generate every zero-sum vector. On the $D_4$ orbit,
transport $F$ to any pair. Conjugation preserves its flip bits because
$\varphi$ has abelian image. Pair the nonzero flip bits, cancel them,
and use single-cell rotations for the residual orientations.
Each of these words acts within its orbit, so the factors can be
generated independently. Consequently
\[
|K|=3^{32-4}4^{552}\frac{8^{192}}2 .
\]

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:structure}]
Necessity follows from the orbit decomposition, parity homomorphism,
local coordinate condition, and two invariant sums.
For sufficiency, choose a legal word whose parity equals that of the
input. The residual position permutation is even on each orbit, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:pure} realizes it independently on every orbit.
Together these words give a group element with the same position
permutation as the input. Applying its inverse to the input fixes the
positions and preserves the orientation conditions. By the kernel
description the residual state lies in $K$, so the input is reachable.

The same position argument identifies $P$. Each parity vector in
$V$ has a fiber of size $\prod_i|\Alt(\Omega_i)|$, giving the stated
order of $P$. Multiplying by $|K|$ gives the order of $G_3$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Constructive orientation correction}
Once positions are solved, the heuristic phase searches conjugates
of position-fixing tools, including single-cell turns, differences
of tools with identical position action, commutators, and stored
$D_4$ words. It accepts only moves that strictly decrease the sum of
orientation distances to the solved state. This nonnegative integer
potential ensures termination, even if the heuristic stalls.

The completion phase first applies available single-cell corrections.
This solves the $C_4$ orbits and leaves only flipped sites on the
$D_4$ orbit. On each $C_3$ orbit, choose two twisted sites and use
breadth-first search on ordered pairs to transport $P_\Omega$ to them.
One or two applications solve the first site, and the number of
twisted sites strictly decreases. The zero-sum constraint excludes
a final lone twist. On the $D_4$ orbit, pair the flipped sites and
transport $F$ to cancel each pair of flips. Single-cell rotations
then solve the pair. The number of flipped sites decreases by two
each time. All these words fix positions.
The implementation reports violations of the parity or orientation
sum conditions when encountered.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Normalize orbit parity, solve positions orbit by orbit, and solve the
remaining orientations by the terminating completion procedure.
Every phase outputs legal atomic moves, so their concatenation solves
the input.
\end{proof}

\section{Replay and move counts}
Search uses a sparse integer simulator. The solver replays its output
in the puzzle engine from a copy of the input, checks legality before
each move, and verifies the final positions and orientations of all
8,000 cells. Any mismatch or illegal move fails replay.
A separate comparison checked the sparse simulator against the engine
over six trials of 40 legal moves, checking all 8,000 cells.

Seven runs with the earlier implementation produced solutions of
111,830--424,519 moves in 112.1--284.2 seconds. For the worst-case
shortest solution length, the number of words of length at most $d$ is
\[
1+M+\cdots+M^d=\frac{M^{d+1}-1}{M-1},\qquad M=3591.
\]
If this count is smaller than the number of states being counted,
some state requires more than $d$ moves.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
States counted & $\log_{10}$ of count & Lower bound\\
\midrule
$\prod_i\Alt(\Omega_i)$ (earlier bound)&11394.834&3206\\
Position group $P$&11399.349&3207\\
Full group $G_3$&11918.138&3353\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus God's number in the standard atomic-move metric is at least
3,353. The measured solver outputs are individual solutions of
unknown optimality.

\section{October 10, 2026 revision}
The October 4 draft left two gaps. For orientations it computed the
single-cell freedom groups, then assumed a descending move would always
be available. The new argument establishes the joint constraints and the
orientation kernel, and implements a terminating completion phase.
For positions the draft inferred the required pure conjugates directly
from the induced alternating groups. The revised proof verifies
orbit-local 3-cycles separately. It stores the derived cycles for the 31
orbits and falls back to exact search when greedy progress stops.
The timing and move counts above were measured on the earlier implementation.

\section{Relationship to Level 2}
The companion document \code{level2-completeness.tex} characterizes
reachable Level 2 states and gives the group order using corresponding
parity and orientation invariants, pure cycles, and two-site tools.
For Level 2, transitivity on ordered triples is checked by exhaustive
search. For Level 3, primitivity and Jordan's theorem give transitivity
through the alternating group.
