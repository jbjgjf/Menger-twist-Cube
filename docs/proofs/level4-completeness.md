# Level 4 Completeness, Group Order, and General Level-$n$ Legal Move Counts

The scope of this document is the `standard` move system. In the atomic-move metric, each $90^\circ$, $-90^\circ$, and $180^\circ$ turn of a legal target counts as 1 move.
Global cube rotations are not quotiented out: we distinguish all cell positions and all 24 cube orientations. We make no claim of shortest-path optimality for the constructive solver.

To compute the orientation holonomy group on an orbit, we fix a reference rotation $r_y$ from the base site to each site $y$ and close the subgroup generated by $r_y^{-1} g r_x$ over all directed transitions $(x \to y,\text{ rotation } g)$.

## 2. Independent Alternating Groups

**Lemma.** Let $G$ be a permutation group generated by 3-cycles on a finite set $\Omega$. If the 3-uniform hypergraph of their 3-element supports is connected on $\Omega$, then $G = \operatorname{Alt}(\Omega)$.

*Proof.* Merge the supports one by one along a connected ordering. Suppose we have already generated $\operatorname{Alt}(S)$ on a subset $S$ of size at least 3 (the base case of a single 3-cycle gives $A_3$ on its 3 points). If the next 3-cycle support intersects $S$ in 2 points, conjugating it by elements of $\operatorname{Alt}(S)$ yields all 3-cycles on $S \cup \{x\}$, generating $\operatorname{Alt}(S \cup \{x\})$. If the new support intersects $S$ in only 1 point, conjugating and taking commutators between the new 3-cycle and an existing 3-cycle in $\operatorname{Alt}(S)$ produces a 3-cycle intersecting $S$ in 2 points, reducing to the previous case and absorbing the two new points in succession. Repeating this along a spanning walk of supports yields $\operatorname{Alt}(\Omega)$. $\square$

For a pure 3-cycle $T$ on an orbit $\Omega$, the connected components of the support hypergraph of all $G$-conjugates of $T$ form a $G$-invariant equivalence relation on $\Omega$.
Starting from the two edges connecting the 3-element support of $T$, the verifier closes their images under all atomic generators using a union-find structure.
Because all $m = |\Omega|$ sites merge into a single connected component, the Lemma implies that the position action of the normal closure of $T$ contains $\operatorname{Alt}(m)$.
Moreover, because $T$ and all of its conjugates fix every other orbit pointwise in both position and orientation, we obtain **$\operatorname{Alt}(m)$ independently on every orbit**.
Note that merely observing $\operatorname{Alt}(m)$ in each single-orbit projection would not suffice to establish this direct product.

Consequently, the order of the induced position group $P$ is

$$
 P=2^{66}\prod_{O}\frac{|O|!}{2}.
$$

One inclusion follows from the independent alternating subgroups together with the 66-dimensional parity image; the reverse inclusion follows from orbit preservation and the fact that the orbit-parity homomorphism has rank 66.

## 3. Orientation Kernel and Group Order

Consider states whose positions are fixed at the identity. On each $C_3$ orbit of size $m$, the sum of twists is $0 \pmod 3$, bounding the orientation kernel factor by at most $3^{m-1}$.
Transporting the pure two-site twist tool across ordered pairs of sites generates all zero-sum twist vectors, achieving $3^{m-1}$.
Because conjugation by the reference transport rotations acts by inner automorphisms of $C_3$ and $C_3$ is abelian, transport preserves the signs of the local twist increments.

On each $C_4$ orbit, every site admits an independent single-cell roll, giving the full orientation kernel factor $C_4^m$.
On the single $D_4$ orbit, every site likewise admits an independent $C_4$ roll subgroup, while the quotient $D_4 / C_4 \cong C_2$ restricts the number of flipped sites to be even.
Combining the transported pure two-site flip tool with the independent single-cell rolls generates all even-flip orientation states, yielding an orientation kernel factor of order $8^m / 2$.
Because all orientation tools have pure support confined to their respective orbits, the orbit factors of the orientation kernel are completely independent.

Therefore, the exact order of the full Level 4 move group $G$ is

$$
 |G|=2^{66}
 (8!/2)^8(24!/2)^{2124}(96!/2)^{855}
 (384!/2)^{60}(768!/2)(1536!/2)^2
 \cdot3^{56}4^{10488}\frac{8^{768}}2.
$$

$$
 \log_{10}|G|=244936.58485676695\ldots
$$

In base 10, $|G|$ has 244,937 digits. Rather than relying on rounded floating-point logarithms to determine diameter bounds, the verifier compares the exact big-integer group order directly against the geometric-series word counts.

## 4. Constructive Completeness

1. Cancel the input orbit parities using the 66-dimensional parity basis. This step always succeeds on reachable states.
2. Decompose the remaining even permutation on each orbit into 3-cycles. The solver shortens long cycles two sites at a time and pairs up any remaining disjoint transpositions, requiring at most $\lfloor m / 2 \rfloor$ 3-cycles on an orbit of size $m$.
3. Realize each required 3-cycle using the orbit's independent pure 3-cycle tools. Because pure tools leave all other orbits invariant, the orbits can be solved in any order.
4. On $C_3$ orbits, cancel nonzero twists two sites at a time; each step strictly decreases the number of twisted sites, and the zero-sum invariant guarantees that a single isolated twist never remains.
   On the $D_4$ orbit, cancel flips in pairs; even flip parity guarantees termination, after which residual $C_4$ rotations are cleared by one single-cell roll per site.

**Realizing a 3-cycle does not require searching over ordered triples.** Under left-to-right word application, if $A$ and $B$ are pure 3-cycles with $A : a \mapsto b$ and $B : c \mapsto b$ whose supports intersect solely at $b$, their commutator is

$$
 [A,B]=ABA^{-1}B^{-1}=(a\ b\ c).
$$

To construct $(a\ b\ c)$, we first transport a stored candidate to obtain a pure 3-cycle $A$ mapping $a \mapsto b$. If the third point of $A$ happens to be $c$, then $A$ (or $A^{-1}$) is already the desired 3-cycle.
Otherwise, let $x$ be the third point of $A$. We then construct a second pure 3-cycle $B$ mapping $c \mapsto b$ whose third point avoids both $a$ and $x$.
Having at least 3 stored candidates that share the same directed base edge $(u \to v)$ while having distinct third points guarantees that at least one candidate's third point avoids the two forbidden sites $\{a, x\}$ after transport.
The existence of at least 38 such candidates on every one of the 3,050 orbits is verified deterministically from the shipped tool data and fixed conjugate expansion.

Because $\operatorname{Alt}(m)$ is 2-transitive for $m \ge 8$, any directed edge can be transported to the common base edge of the candidate pool.
The implementation performs an **exhaustive** breadth-first search over the ordered-pair graph on every orbit—which has at most $1{,}536 \times 1{,}535 = 2{,}357{,}760$ vertices—with no empirical depth cutoffs and no fallback to ordered-triple search.
Consequently, every required correction word is constructed in finitely many steps, solving every reachable state.

## 5. God's Number Bounds

With $M = 44{,}784$ legal atomic moves, the number of words of length at most $d$ is bounded by

$$
 B_M(d)=1+M+\cdots+M^d=\frac{M^{d+1}-1}{M-1}.
$$

Whenever $B_M(d) < |G|$, the diameter of $G$ exceeds $d$. Exact big-integer comparison yields the lower bound

$$
 \operatorname{diam}(G)\ge52662.
$$

An explicit **constructive upper bound** is obtained by a separate exhaustive diameter analysis of the transport graphs. For each orbit $i$, let $K_i$ be the maximum word length among the stored common-edge 3-cycle candidates, and let $D_i$ be the maximum BFS distance from any ordered pair to the common base edge. A single transported 3-cycle has length at most $K_i + 2D_i$, so its commutator has length at most $4K_i + 8D_i$. Thus the position phase requires at most

$$
 U_P=\sum_i\lfloor m_i/2\rfloor(4K_i+8D_i)
$$

moves. The orientation phase requires at most $2(m_i - 1)$ transported two-site twists on each $C_3$ orbit, at most $m_i / 2$ transported two-site flips on the $D_4$ orbit, and at most 11,256 direct single-cell rolls across all $C_4$ and $D_4$ sites.
Parity normalization adds at most 70 moves. The exact per-orbit values $D_i$ and the resulting totals are recorded in `research/level4/out/transport-certificate.json`.
This upper bound is an analytical worst-case guarantee over all reachable states, not an empirical maximum over a finite sample of solver trajectories.

Exhaustively inspecting all 23,099,392 ordered-pair states across all 3,050 orbits shows $\max_i D_i = 8$ for the position tools, giving $U_P = 23{,}609{,}664$, orientation phase bound $U_O = 35{,}336$, and parity phase bound 70. Therefore,

$$
 \boxed{52662\le\operatorname{diam}(G)\le23645070}.
$$

## 6. Legal Move Count for General Level $n$

This section applies not just up to the UI limit of Level 5, but to any positive integer $n$ under the same recursive Menger sponge definition.
Let $S_n$ be the set of occupied sites at Level $n$, with $|S_n| = 20^n$, and let $R_n$ denote the number of legal single-cell roll targets.

**Geometric Lemma.** An axial rotation of an edge sub-block—and likewise each of the 3 axial slab rotations within that sub-block—is legal for all angles $\in \{90^\circ, -90^\circ, 180^\circ\}$ if and only if the sub-block has no occupied face-adjacent neighbor block in either of the two directions perpendicular to the rotation axis.
Whenever such a face-adjacent block is occupied, corner cells on the shared face collide immediately as rotation begins, rendering both $\pm 90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ turns illegal.

*Proof.* A rotating square of side length $s$ has circumradius $\sqrt{2}\,s/2$.
The distance from the rotation axis to the inner corner of a diagonally adjacent block is also $\sqrt{2}\,s/2$, so rotating cells make at most boundary contact with diagonal neighbors and never reach more distant blocks.
Distinct integer layers along the rotation axis have disjoint interiors. Conversely, if a perpendicular face-adjacent block is occupied, the Menger sponge construction always populates the corner cells of that shared face in both blocks, so the rotating corner cells immediately sweep into the interior of the stationary neighbor block.
The same holds for slabs because every axial layer of a Menger block retains its four corner cells. This criterion is completely independent of internal holes at finer levels. $\square$

Fixing one of the 12 lowest-level edge slots (say along the $X$-axis with negative $Y$ and $Z$ offsets), its two outward perpendicular face neighbors are empty if and only if the parent site $p \in S_{n-1}$ satisfies $p - e_Y \notin S_{n-1}$ and $p - e_Z \notin S_{n-1}$.
Writing $A_{n-1}$ for the number of such parent sites, symmetry across the 12 edge slots gives $R_n = 12 A_{n-1}$.

For $S_m$, let $B_m$ be the number of sites having an occupied neighbor in a fixed negative coordinate direction, and let $C_m$ be the number of sites having occupied neighbors in both of two fixed negative coordinate directions.
The outer face of $S_m$ is a Sierpiński carpet of branching factor 8; letting $b_m$ denote the number of 1-axis adjacent pairs within that outer face, all three quantities satisfy linear recurrences with initial values $B_0 = b_0 = C_0 = 0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
 B_m&=20B_{m-1}+8\cdot8^{m-1}=\tfrac23(20^m-8^m),\\
 b_m&=8b_{m-1}+4\cdot3^{m-1}=\tfrac45(8^m-3^m),\\
 C_m&=20C_{m-1}+16b_{m-1}+2\cdot3^{m-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here the second term of $C_m$ counts sites where exactly one of the two negative steps crosses a parent-block boundary ($2 \times 8$ parent-face contacts $\times\, b_{m-1}$ in-face pairs), and the third term counts sites where both steps cross parent-block boundaries (2 top-level corner triples $\times\, 3^{m-1}$ edge sites).
By inclusion–exclusion,

$$
 A_m=20^m-2B_m+C_m
 =\frac5{51}20^m+\frac4{15}8^m+\frac{54}{85}3^m,
$$

which yields the closed form

$$
 \boxed{R_n=\frac{100\cdot20^{n-1}+272\cdot8^{n-1}+648\cdot3^{n-1}}{85}}.
$$

At recursion depth $d$, the number of legal extension block targets is $R_d$ and the number of legal slab targets at depth $d + 1/2$ is $3R_d$.
All frame (slice) targets are legal, and there are $F_n = 9(3^n - 1)/2$ of them.
Multiplying by the 3 non-identity angles per legal target gives the exact total number of legal atomic moves at Level $n$:

$$
 \boxed{M_n=3\left(\frac{9(3^n-1)}2+R_n+4\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}R_d\right)}.
$$

Evaluating these formulas for $n = 1, \dots, 5$ gives $R_1, \dots, R_5 = 12, 72, 744, 11256, 201960$ and $M_1, \dots, M_5 = 63, 468, 3591, 44784, 754155$.
These values match the full engine enumerations for Levels 1–4, all 348,624 candidate extension angle checks at Level 4, and an independent boundary-enumeration check of $R_n$ through Level 5.

## 7. Asymptotic Diameter Lower Bound for General $n$

Each legal single-cell roll rotates a single cell by an element of order 4 while leaving all other cells fixed. Because single-cell rolls at distinct sites have disjoint supports, they commute and are completely independent, so the move group $G_n$ contains an isomorphic copy of $C_4^{R_n}$ for every $n \ge 1$.
Thus $|G_n| \ge 4^{R_n}$.
Since $R_n = \Theta(20^n)$ and $M_n = \Theta(20^n)$ by the closed forms above, comparing $M_n^d$ against $4^{R_n}$ gives the unconditional asymptotic diameter lower bound

$$
 \boxed{\operatorname{diam}(G_n)=\Omega(20^n/n)}.
$$

Crucially, this general-$n$ lower bound does not rely on any unproved extrapolation that all position orbits contain alternating groups for general $n$.
The exact Level 4 group order and the 52,662 move lower bound established in Sections 3 and 5 are stronger, finite-level results obtained via explicit machine-checked certificates.
We make no claims from this calculation alone regarding God's number for other twisty puzzles in the literature.

Finally, since $R_n / 20^n \to 1/17$ and $M_n / 20^n \to 69/323$ as $n \to \infty$, the leading asymptotic constant satisfies

$$
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n\,\operatorname{diam}(G_n)}{20^n}\ge\frac{\log 4}{17\log 20}.
$$
