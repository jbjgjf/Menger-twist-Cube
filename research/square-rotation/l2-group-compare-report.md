## 3. Measured: GF(2) Subspace Spanned by Orbit Parities

Comparison methodology (important): Because $G$-values and $H$-values must not be compared on incompatible partitions if $G$-orbits and $H$-orbits differ, we **use the $H$-orbit partition as the common coarse partition**. We verified that every $H$-orbit is a disjoint union of $G$-orbits and re-expressed each $G$-generator's parity vector as the XOR sum of the parities of the constituent $G$-orbits within each $H$-orbit. The code also asserts that this equals the parity of the restriction directly on the $H$-orbit. Because the two orbit partitions coincide identically in Level 2, this projection is the identity map here, but we record the methodology explicitly.

| | Partition | Rank | Subspace size | Distinct nonzero generator parity vectors |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| G | H-orbits (11) | 5 | 32 | 6 |
| H | H-orbits (11) | 6 | 64 | 8 |
| G | G-orbits (11, native) | 5 | 32 | 6 |

**The parity rank increases from 5 to 6.** This is the sole invariant difference found between $G$ and $H$ at Level 2.

## 4. Proved: H Is Strictly Larger Than G (Verdict **B**)

Among the 54 additional atomic moves, **24 moves have orbit parity vectors lying outside the $\mathbf F_2$-subspace spanned by $G$**.

| Outlier parity bitmask | Orbits whose parity flips | Corresponding atomic moves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 512 | G09 (CE, 24 cells) | Inner ring on layer $\pm3$ planes (12 cells, bounding box side 5): 6 targets × angles $\pm90^\circ$ = 12 moves |
| 162 | G01 + G05 + G07 (all CE, 24 cells) | Outer ring on layer $\pm3$ planes (20 cells, bounding box side 9): 6 targets × angles $\pm90^\circ$ = 12 moves |

Structure of the proof: The orbit parity map $\varepsilon: G \to \mathbf F_2^{11}$ is a group homomorphism whose image equals the subspace spanned by the parity vectors of the generators of $G$. Each of the 24 moves above (i) preserves every $G$-orbit so that $\varepsilon$ is well-defined on it, and (ii) takes an $\varepsilon$-value outside $\operatorname{im}(\varepsilon|_G)$, proving it is not an element of $G$. Because the move group acts freely on the solved state (all cells are distinguishable, so a group element is uniquely determined by the state it produces from the solved state), applying any of these 24 moves to the solved state produces a state that is **reachable in $H$ but unreachable in $G$**. Hence the set of reachable states strictly increases.

This computation was independently re-implemented in a second language (Python) to verify the orbit decomposition, parity vectors, rank, and subspace membership, confirming full agreement (11 orbits, rank $5\to6$, 24 outlier moves, and orbit preservation).

Remark: Because the difference (XOR) between the outlier bitmasks 512 and 162 lies inside $\operatorname{im}(\varepsilon|_G)$ (consistent with the rank increasing by only 1, from 5 to 6), both families of outlier moves represent the same nontrivial coset modulo $\operatorname{im}(\varepsilon|_G)$.

## 5. Undetermined (as of 2026-09-18): Membership of the Remaining 30 Moves

The remaining 30 moves (all angles of the 4-cell inner ring and 12-cell outer ring on layer-0 planes, together with the $180^\circ$ turns of both rings on layer-$\pm3$ planes) do not violate any of the invariants tested in this initial script:

- Preservation of $G$-orbits: 0 violations
- Single-cell $(\text{position}\times\text{orientation})$ reachability (whether the state resulting from applying 1 move to the solved state is consistent with the single-cell automaton of $G$): 0 violations
- Membership of the orbit parity vector in the subspace spanned by $G$: all 30 moves lie inside the subspace

A constructive membership search via bidirectional BFS (forward 2-move ball of 115,030 states from the solved state × backward 2-move ball of 115,030 states using inverse atomic moves) established that **none of the 30 moves can be written as a word of length $\le4$ in $G$**. This provides a word-length lower bound, not a proof of non-membership.

Because all 48 signed coordinate-permutation symmetries of the cube preserve both the existing atomic move set and the additional atomic move set as sets (verified across all $48\times522$ moves), the cube symmetry group normalizes $G$, and membership in $G$ is constant across each symmetry class. The 54 additional moves partition into 8 symmetry classes, of which 6 classes (30 moves) remained undetermined at this stage. With `--deep-search`, all 30 moves were searched individually up to word length 4 without relying on symmetry reduction, yielding the same result.

## 6. Conjectured (Not a Proof; Disproved in Subsequent Addendum)

- At the time of this script run, we conjectured that the 30 undetermined moves also did not belong to $G$, on the weak heuristic grounds that layer-0 rings belong to the same geometric family as layer-$\pm3$ rings and might violate a finer invariant beyond orbit parity. However, no tested invariant separated them.
- For the $180^\circ$ moves on layer $\pm3$, since $a^2 \in G$ does not imply $a \in G$, it remained entirely possible for the squares of the outlier quarter-turns to lie in $G$.

Resolving membership required applying a Level 2 solver to each of the 54 single-move states or analyzing the group via Schreier–Sims (which was subsequently carried out, proving that all 30 moves in fact belong to $G$ and $[H:G]=2$; see the top note).

## 7. Summary of Verdict (as of 2026-09-18)

**Verdict B: Proved that the set of reachable states strictly increases from $G$ to $H$.** The decisive invariant is that 24 of the additional moves have orbit parity vectors lying outside the parity image of $G$. Position orbits and single-cell orientation freedoms coincide identically between $G$ and $H$ and thus do not separate the two groups (conversely, identical orbits and single-cell freedoms must not be mistaken for $G = H$). Individual membership of the remaining 30 moves was undetermined in this script and subsequently resolved in the affirmative (see the top note).
